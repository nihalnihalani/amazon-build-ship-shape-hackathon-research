### 2.3 User interviews and scenario tests (nothing assumed about results)

**Who.** Five to eight people in two rounds: adults who have been told by a clinician to practise speaking louder (if any can be reached), household listeners or caregivers, and, failing those, adults of any age who agree to act the two roles. One speech-language pathologist for asynchronous written feedback if a contact exists; none is confirmed.

**How to recruit, ethically.** Personal network first; then local Parkinson's support groups or SLP contacts with a plain-language note (no group has been contacted and none is named here because none was verified in reviewed sources); online communities only with moderator permission. The invitation says what the session is (a 20-minute practice-aid comparison), what is and is not collected (notes and numbers; no audio recording; no health data stored; nothing identifiable in any public file), that participants can stop at any time, that no payment is involved and nothing is being sold, and that the app is a practice aid, not therapy. Written or recorded verbal consent before starting. Participants are not filmed; the developer demos personally in the video and the README says so. No participant is quoted by name.

**What to ask (scenario test, then interview).**
1. Sit on the sofa, 3 m from the TV, phone on its mark. Do the calibration and three phrases with the remote only. Then do the same three phrases holding a phone meter at arm's length.
2. Which did you prefer, and why? Could you read the arc and the text from the sofa? (This is the one-day falsification from `distinction_tested_or_untested`; if both participants prefer the phone, the TV premise weakens to "convenient".)
3. Was anything too fast, too small or unclear? Did user-paced advance feel right?
4. Listener role: standing at the far wall, could you tell when a phrase "reached" you? Would you press a Heard/Not heard button (family mode, core since 4 Oct, and the fallback's headline control)? Would one agreed cue word feel helpful or like policing?
5. How often would you realistically do a 10-15 minute session, and what would make you stop?
6. What is missing for you or your family member?

**Recording results.** `research/user_feedback.md`: date, participant count, roles, what was observed, what changed because of it. If fewer than five people take part, the file says so. No testimonial is written for the video or the Devpost text that did not happen.

### 6.3 The one failure/recovery case, specified

- **Trigger:** the phone page compares the pre-window noise floor with the session's calibrated floor. If the floor is above a fixed margin (value to be set from the day-3 calibration tests, not yet measured), the page sends `{"state":"noisy"}` instead of samples.
- **TV behaviour:** shows "Room too noisy. Try again when it is quiet.", does not score, does not auto-advance, and waits for OK.
- **Recovery on camera:** noise stops, OK pressed, window re-runs, phrase scored.
- **Why this case:** it is deterministic, reproducible in one take, and demonstrates the design stance "refuse to guess" that answers the technical-judge risk "uncalibrated phone mic" (concept `judge_rejection_risks`).
- **Backup failure case** (only if the noise gate cannot be triggered cleanly on camera): switch the phone to airplane mode for two seconds; the TV shows "Phone link lost. Reconnecting..." (the string in the FTV-4 spec §2.3 and §3) and the transport badge, the open window is cancelled and not scored; reconnect and continue. This depends on the reconnect logic planned for the Tue 13 Oct row actually working by the shoot.

### 7.2 Inspiration

### 7.3 What it does

[PLACEHOLDER: this describes the planned prototype. Before pasting, check every step against the build and the final video; delete or mark "proposed" anything that was cut (K4 order) or not shown.]

1. **Pair.** The TV shows a room code and QR. An Android phone opens an HTTPS page (no install), joins the room, and is placed on a taped mark beside the TV, at least 2.5 m from the sofa. The person practising never touches it again. A badge on the TV shows whether the phone is measuring.
2. **Calibrate every session.** Five seconds of room noise floor, then one calibration phrase from the seat. This sets today's personal far-side band, so phone gain drift or a moved mark cannot carry over between sessions.
3. **Warm up.** A sustained "ahh" with a seconds counter (voice-activity detection on the phone), ended by OK. No timeout.
4. **Practise household phrases.** Ten phrases typed once by the family on the phone page. For each, the TV shows big text and a countdown ring, stays silent during the measurement window, and draws a large arc of the far-mic level relative to today's baseline. A deterministic "reached the far side" band turns the arc green. The user advances with OK only. On three phrases per session the arc is hidden (a blind check): the person first says how loud the phrase felt, then the TV shows what reached the far side.
5. **Refuse bad input.** If the room is noisy, the TV says "Room too noisy, try again" and scores nothing.
6. **Summarise.** Phrases that reached the far side, how the blind checks compared with what the person felt, longest sustained "ahh", and a 7-day chart. If a family member listens from the far side and taps Heard or Not heard on the far phone, the summary also counts how often the meter and the listener agreed. [PLACEHOLDER: add "and a caregiver text export from the phone page" only if that stretch shipped.]

Only levels (numbers) and the phrases the family typed travel from the phone to the TV. No audio is recorded, sent or stored anywhere. Adaptation is deterministic (median of the last five sessions' peaks plus 1 dB), and cross-day loudness comparison is shown only if the Android AGC-off monotonicity test passed; otherwise the week shows time-based metrics (sessions completed, longest phonation) which gain control cannot corrupt. Footer on every screen: "Not a medical device. Does not diagnose or treat any condition."

## 9. Friction-log template

**Why keep one.** Rules §4 lists the optional friction log (task, steps, expected vs actual, severity, workaround, actionable suggestion) and Rules §6 says it can earn **up to a 10% bonus**; Amazon's internal review team recommends the bonus during Stage 1 and the Stage 2 panel applies it to the final spreadsheet score (brief §6, M32). How the 10% is computed is not published (brief §12 open questions). Serial entrants' visible submissions include friction findings (brief §10), so the bar is real.

**How to keep it.**
1. Record friction **the moment it happens**, in `docs/FRICTION_LOG.md`, before looking for the workaround. Memory of friction decays within a day.
2. One row per incident. Give each row an ID (`FL-001`, …) so product-feedback answers and the Devpost text can cite it.
3. Always capture the environment: macOS build, Vega SDK build number (`vega sdk list-remote` after install), RN track (0.72 or 0.83), package versions, Node version, phone model and browser version, AWS region.
4. Paste the exact error text and the doc URL you were following. Screenshots go in `docs/friction/` with the row ID in the filename.
5. Daily close-out (about 10 minutes, every build day Mon 5 Oct – Wed 21 Oct 2026 IST): re-read the day's rows, fill the severity, workaround and "actionable improvement" columns, and mark rows that are now resolved. Thu 22 Oct 2026 IST: final edit and copy of the strongest rows into the Devpost product-feedback answer.
6. Never invent, backdate or embellish a row. If something expected to fail did not fail, that is not friction; note it in product feedback as "what worked".
7. Record friction with **every** tool, including AWS services, browsers and the Devpost form itself if it blocks you.

**Severity scale (team definition, state it in the file):** S1 blocker (no workaround, work stopped); S2 major (workaround cost > 1 hour or changed the design); S3 minor (workaround < 1 hour); S4 cosmetic or docs-only.
