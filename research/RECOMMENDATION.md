## 1. The recommendation

Build **FTV-4 "Across the Room: the far-side check"**: a React Native for Vega app for Fire TV (Vega OS), demonstrated on the Vega Virtual Device, that turns the TV into a remote-only practice stand for adults who have been told by a clinician to practise speaking louder at home, together with the household listener or caregiver who shares the living room. The core workflow is a daily 10–15 minute session: an Android phone lies on a taped mark beside the TV, at least 2.5 m from the sofa, as the far-field microphone and is never touched; the user presses OK on the remote; the TV runs a 5-second room-noise check and one calibration phrase from the sofa; then a 30-second sustained "ahh" with a seconds counter; then ten household phrases typed once by the family, each shown as big text with a silent countdown while a large arc fills from the far microphone and turns green when the level reaching the listener's end of the room clears that person's own far-side band; the user advances with OK only; Back opens a session summary, a 7-day chart and a caregiver card with a text export. Only numbers (plus the phrases the family typed) travel from the phone to the TV, over an API Gateway WebSocket relay (ap-south-1) with a 250 ms long-poll fallback; there is no audio upload and no AI model in the loop. **Primary track:** Fire TV. **Mini challenges:** Open Source (MIT "ten-foot-meter" arc component and "phone-mic-meter" browser module with jest fixtures) as the stronger pairing; AWS Builder as a free secondary entry (API Gateway WebSocket + Lambda + DynamoDB connections table, Polly prompts in S3/CloudFront; development with Kiro Crew or Claude Code on Bedrock). **The dependency that must work first** is the Vega toolchain on this Mac: Rosetta 2 (the [Vega SDK install doc](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/vega/0.24/install-vega-sdk) says upgrading from macOS 26 to macOS 27 removes Rosetta and leaves Vega tools broken, and asks for 20 GB free disk), Node 20 via nvm (this machine has v25; the install doc asks for Node 18.x or later and the [Vega sample READMEs](https://github.com/AmazonAppDev/vega-tv-interfaces-sample) pin v18–v20, so 20 is a precaution), Vega SDK 0.24, a booting Vega Virtual Device and the official [`react-native-multi-tv-helloworld`](https://github.com/AmazonAppDev/react-native-multi-tv-helloworld) Vega target rendering. Second in line, on the same day and with no AWS account, is the reversal test: Android Chrome `autoGainControl:false` must give monotonic relative loudness readings at 2.5–3 m. If the toolchain fails there is no Fire TV entry; if the microphone test fails the concept switches to its Listener Edition fallback (§5.4) or to the runner-up (§4), depending on the date (§5.2).

Thesis sentence for the video, README and Devpost text (proposed): *"Phone voice apps measure your voice at arm's length; Across the Room measures it where the listener sits, 3 metres away, and you control everything with the TV remote."*

**Amazon-native features checked.** A live amazon.com search with the Fire TV filter for "speech therapy voice exercise" returned "No results for speech therapy voice exercise in Apps & Games" (`.firecrawl/oct4/rt2-ftv4-amazon-firetv-speech-apps.md` line 164; one query, US store). The [JMIR 2026 co-design paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12917486/) states there are no Alexa skills for adults with Parkinson's or dysarthria. Alexa+ on Fire TV (free in the US since 19 Aug 2026, per [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/alexa-plus-fire-tv-free-ai)) does conversational search and Q&A, not voice practice ([TRENDS_AND_RIVALS.md §3A](TRENDS_AND_RIVALS.md#3a-amazons-own-capabilities-do-not-rebuild-these-as-the-thesis)). Fire OS screen mirroring could put a phone dB-meter on a TV for nothing; Vega mirroring support is not verified in reviewed sources.

**The distinction, and whether it is tested.** The far-side geometry (measure where the listener sits, display on the TV, remote-only, phone never touched, caregiver summary) is **absent from every rival page reviewed** (Loud and Clear store and site, Speak Up store page, the MARS review of three free apps, the Tactus Therapy list, the Fire TV Appstore search, the Alexa skill store search). It is **untested**: no rival app was installed, no clinician or person with Parkinson's has been interviewed, and the planned one-day falsification (TV at 3 m versus tablet at arm's length with two volunteers) has not been run. The acoustic claim that far-microphone relative level tracks what a listener hears is an inference; the day-one monotonicity test validates the sensor, not intelligibility. "Not verified in reviewed sources" means we did not find it; it never means the rival lacks it.

**Why there is still a credible opportunity.**
- The need is dated and multi-sourced: the JMIR 2026 paper lists wanted features (visual biofeedback, volume feedback, progress monitoring, reminders); the MARS review found the free apps patient-only, with subjective progress and reviewers who "would not pay to use any of these apps", so a free TV form factor competes on convenience, not price.
- Documented rival complaints are answered by cheap design decisions: a target volume that a reviewer found too high with the mic 15–18 inches from the iPad ([Speak Up store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/speak-up-for-parkinsons/id784117908)) becomes a personal far-side band; an SLP's complaint that the new version does not give older patients enough time to repeat each phrase ([Loud and Clear store review](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/loud-and-clear/id1491171131)) becomes OK-only advance; a phrase bank called too small (same Speak Up review) becomes a household bank typed by the family. These complaints are paraphrased store reviews, single anecdotes.
- Platform facts are verified from Amazon and AWS pages: Fire TV is exempt from the runtime hook; the VVD is named in the rules; API Gateway has ap-south-1 endpoints and a 7,200 s WebSocket connection quota ([AWS General Reference](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/apigateway.html), saved `.firecrawl/oct4/rt2ftv4-aws-gr-apigateway.md` lines 452–528); `@amazon-devices/react-native-w3cmedia` documents an `Audio` component for MP3 ([Vega API docs](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/vega-api/0.24/README.amazon-devices_react-native-w3cmedia)); the Kepler a11y package exposes UI scale ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@amazon-devices/kepler-a11y-settings-interface-turbo)). The VVD's lack of a microphone is irrelevant because the phone is the microphone.
- Visible crowding on this theme is zero: no voice, speech, loudness or Parkinson's repo among roughly 46 visible Fire TV repos as of 4 Oct ([`_working/github.md`](_working/github.md); `.firecrawl/oct4/gh-new-since-oct2-a.json`). This is a lower bound from public GitHub only.

**What would make the distinction collapse.**
1. A rival is found to offer a far-field or listener-position mode, a TV output or a caregiver view. Only store and marketing pages were read.
2. The Android Chrome AGC-off test fails, so "reached the far side" measures the gain controller, not the voice; the far-side claim then shrinks to per-session relative peaks (the fallback in §5.4).
3. A judge reads the form-factor move as "the same free app on a bigger screen". The red team's judge lens expected exactly this unless the first sentence and the first 20 seconds of the video are the geometry.
4. A Vega stick is shown to mirror a phone dB-meter app natively, making the TV display trivially available.
5. A visible or invisible entrant ships voice practice on Fire TV before judging; the field grows by roughly 15–20 public repos a day.

**The single assumption that would reverse the choice:** that a phone-browser microphone on Android Chrome with `autoGainControl:false` yields monotonic, repeatable relative loudness readings (within about ±2 dB at a fixed level over ten repeats) at 2.5–3 m, so that "reached the far side" is a measurement and not a reading of the gain controller. MDN's compatibility table ([autoGainControl](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaTrackConstraints/autoGainControl), saved `.firecrawl/oct4/eng2-mdn-autogaincontrol.md` lines 234 and 295) lists "Full support" from Chrome Android 67 and "No support" on Safari, Safari on iOS and WebView on iOS, and says a plain boolean "will not fail if this can't be done", so support is best-effort and unmeasured; the OS microphone pipeline may add its own AGC. If this fails on two Android phones or browsers by Tue 6 Oct evening IST (before TV app code is sunk), FTV-3 becomes the primary on the same Vega toolchain. If it fails on 7–8 Oct, the Listener Edition fallback runs instead. A lesser reversal: if the user owns no Android phone and cannot borrow one, an iPhone cannot be the cross-session microphone, which also tilts the choice to FTV-3. ## 8. Contrarian findings and open questions

### 8.1 Contrarian findings
1. **The round-2 judges' top scorer was killed.** ALX-4 ClearPlate (3.63, "best-evidenced impact in the set") fell to 2.86 after two of three red-team lenses killed it: Amazon markets Alexa+ for a "family's dietary preferences, allergies" ([Amazon 2025 devices event](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-2025-devices-alexa-event-live-updates)), and an entrant created on 3 Oct ([Aakashdeeeep/MCP](https://github.com/Aakashdeeeep/MCP), "Raksha") already ships the same deterministic-gate architecture. Impact evidence does not rescue a native-feature rebuild.
2. **Fire TV, not Alexa+, is where an India-based solo can be fully real.** Alexa+ integration is "None whatsoever" ([45397](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/45397-what-exactly-are-you-looking-for-in-the-alexa-track)); Fire TV apps "can actually fully integrate" ([45396](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/45396-what-exactly-are-you-looking-for-in-the-fire-tv-track)). The two highest-scoring survivors are both Fire TV.
3. **"No AI model in the loop" is a scoring asset here (inference),** given the organizer's "latent capability" remark, even though the event is tagged Machine Learning/AI. It also avoids the Bedrock quota-0 and suspension reports on new accounts ([45416](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/45416-bedrock-blocked-on-a-new-aws-account-all-model-quotas-0-not-adjustable-need-escalation), [45384](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/45384-bedrock-access-suspended-hackathon-project-live-demo-affected)). 4. **The official tie-break favours the runner-up,** yet the base-case choice is FTV-4: tie-breaks apply only after equal totals, and FTV-3's total is dragged by collapsing originality and solo capacity. With two people the ordering flips.
5. **The phone, not the TV, is FTV-4's hidden hardware dependency** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaTrackConstraints/autoGainControl): no `autoGainControl` support on Safari/iOS). The base case silently assumes an Android phone.
6. **Feasibility claims were systematically optimistic** across both rounds: buffers of "4–9 h" and "12 h" turned negative once AWS onboarding, OSS repos, security basics and host wiring were itemised. Treat every hour figure as unmeasured.
7. **Rival tables built from homepages are a recurring failure mode:** FTV-4's original list missed two free dedicated apps (Loud and Clear, Speak Up For Parkinson's); ALX-3 missed Brelly; ALX-4 missed Belay.
8. **"Uncrowded" is a lower-bound statement about public GitHub.** Twenty hackathon-labelled repos were created on 3 Oct alone ([TRENDS_AND_RIVALS.md §7](TRENDS_AND_RIVALS.md#7-visible-hackathon-competition-public-github-lower-bounds)); the gallery is unpublished; the 35,590 registrations on the [event overview](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/) (4 Oct) say nothing about per-track submissions.
9. **Hosting is optional under the FAQ, but any endpoint you point judges at is a 20 Nov obligation** (Testing clause; [45411](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/45411-must-the-hosted-mcp-endpoint-stay-live-through-judging)). For the relay this is cheap, but it needs a budget alarm and a documented local-run path.

## 9. Research limitations

- **The project gallery is unpublished** and private repositories are invisible, so every crowding statement is a lower bound from public GitHub keyword searches (about 363 hackathon-labelled repos on 3 Oct plus 20 created on 3 Oct), classified by description keywords with known errors.
- **Forum answers are clarifications, not rules** (Rules §11.4). Several load-bearing allowances (deterministic simulations, Bee "generated audio", the Android TV emulator) rest on forum or FAQ text that the rules do not contain.
- **Vendor and store pages are mostly undated**; where dates exist they are "last modified" footers. The Loud and Clear rating (4.4 from 53) and version (7.4.1, Sep 14) are retrieval-time snapshots of 4 Oct 2026.
- **All hour, capacity, latency and cost figures are unmeasured estimates** (6.5 h/day, 95–100 h for FTV-4, 150–300 ms warm relay latency, 6–14 s Textract chain). None has been timed on this machine or network.
- **No user interviews, clinician conversations or product tests have been run.** Rival capabilities come from store listings, marketing pages and two peer-reviewed papers read as saved excerpts, not from installing the apps.
- **MDN compatibility tables are documentation claims**, not measurements of the user's phone; the iOS `takePhoto` version (18.4) comes from the 4 Oct red team's reading.
- **Firecrawl rate limits and site blocks:** Reddit bodies were unreadable; Belay's feature page hit a rate limit; the Ring Playground console and the Devpost submission form sit behind logins and were not viewed. The decision pass ran no new live scrapes; it relied on raw pages saved 3–4 Oct under `.firecrawl/` and `.firecrawl/oct4/`.
- **Sources not read are not counted.** Two pages were read only as WebFetch summaries and are marked so in the digests. Single-source anecdotes (the Playground's full-frame zone, the 4K Select OS 1.1 reports, the Bee Watch recording) are labelled as such.
- **Discord was not reviewed** (login required), although the Amazon team is said to be active there ([45368](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/forum_topics/45368-aws-credit-rejections-unclear-template-responses)).
- **The scoring model is the user's own**, not the official rubric; the official rubric weights four criteria equally with a Tech-first tie-break, and the two models differ at the margin (FTV-3 vs FTV-4). Judge scores and red-team revisions are model-generated with three judges and three lenses each; "killed" follows a mechanical two-kill rule. Six concepts (FTV-1, FTV-2, RNG-4, FTV-5, BEE-4, BEE-5) were never red-teamed.
- **Scraped content was treated as data, never as instructions.**
