### Concrete user need (what the evidence actually supports)
- Information arrives through many channels (email, WhatsApp groups, school/sports apps, flyers) and must be noticed, extracted and acted on - stated by Fambot founder (anecdote) and implied by the design of CC, Carly, Cozi, Skylight, Hearth (supply). Repeated across independent vendors = strong **supply** signal; **demand** evidence is mostly founder/company-reported (Google "user requests", Fambot ">1,000 families tested") and not independently measured.
- The second-adult problem (partner/co-parent/caregiver not checking or not acknowledging) appears in vendor copy (Maple, Carly) and in Reddit thread titles/snippets (r/Mommit "Does your partner check the daycare app?", r/Parenting "Dealing with total schedule chaos" - snippet mentions "who's picking up"). Reddit bodies were not readable -> anecdote level only.

### Implementation implication (20-day build)
- "Import school email -> calendar" is commoditised (Alexa+ itself, Skylight, Cozi, Hearth, Google CC, Carly, Fambot). Do **not** lead with extraction.
- Google CC now explicitly claims to keep the family calendar current "even as things change" (official post, Sep 2026), so "auto-update the calendar after a change" is no longer open space either. The narrower open space is **accepted ownership and downstream consistency**: when an event moves, who is now responsible, did they accept, and which downstream items (pickup, carpool family, meal, reminder) are now invalid. Even Carly's own pitch (emailing the other parent and carpool family) shows this is the contested frontier, not a solved one.
- Alexa+ track fit: a self-hosted MCP server exposing `propose_repair`, `request_acceptance`, `commit_plan`, `rollback` over a household plan store; deterministic constraint check (time/travel/ownership) rather than LLM judgment. Simulated Alexa+ in a web app is allowed if MCP host access is limited.
- Bee fit: spoken "I can't do Thursday pickup anymore" as the trigger for a repair - but Bee requires real device data, so plan recording time.

### Why it could be hype rather than durable need
- Multiple launches in one month (CC, Fambot, Ollie) is a **supply wave**, typical of an agent hype cycle; none of the reviewed sources reports retention or paid conversion.
- CC is an opt-in Labs experiment with a waitlist; Fambot is free beta. Pricing intent (Fambot ~Netflix price; Ollie per-message tiers per Carly's page) is not proof of willingness to pay.
- Family-calendar products have existed for over a decade (Cozi); "AI" may be a feature refresh rather than a new need.

### Concrete user needs supported by evidence
- Delivery anxiety and missed deliveries are real but **estimates conflict wildly** (lifetime theft victims: 25% Security.org vs 46% Insurify; annual stolen packages: 37M Security.org vs 104M SafeWise). Every figure comes from a firm that sells or insures security; use only as "commonly reported", never as a precise number.
- The most-used mitigation is behavioural (being home: 46%), not technical; in-home delivery (Amazon Key) is used by only 3% in Security.org's sample -> people manage presence, not locks.
- Caregivers are numerous, local (74% within 20 min) and increasingly high-intensity (AARP) - a strong, non-vendor demand base for "who is coming to the door / who is covering today" coordination.

### Implementation implication (20-day build)
- **Do not build "AI answers the door for deliveries"** - Alexa+ Greetings already does it first-party (supply exists since Dec 2025). A Ring-track project should consume Ring events (simulator allowed) and add something Greetings does not claim: e.g., caregiver/aide arrival verification against an agreed schedule, handoff acknowledgment between family members, an accessible (large-text/visual/captioned) door experience for a resident with hearing or mobility limits, or business arrival queues.
- Consent-first design is mandatory given the 2026 Ring privacy backlash: no face recognition, no neighbour-camera scanning, explicit opt-in for every participant, visible retention limits. Judges will likely notice surveillance-adjacent framing.
- Alexa+ fit: MCP tools such as `expected_visitors_today`, `confirm_arrival`, `escalate_if_no_show` with a web simulator.

### Why it could be hype
- Package-theft statistics are marketing content for security vendors and spike every holiday season; SafeWise itself reports a decline.
- "AI concierge at the door" is a supply-push feature from Amazon; reviewed sources contain no adoption or satisfaction data for Alexa+ Greetings.
- Caregiver tech has a long history of low adoption; reviewed sources show many apps but no evidence any coordination app is widely used.

### Concrete user needs
- Shared-room play without everyone needing a console controller: Netflix's phone-as-controller pattern is now mainstream supply; Amazon's own guidance insists apps stay fully usable with the remote and voice.
- Accessibility at 10 feet (VoiceView, captions, contrast, tactile remote) is explicitly pushed by Amazon in July and September 2026 - a judge-legible "Design" criterion.

### Implementation implication (20-day build)
- A Fire TV entry must run on Fire OS or Vega (device or simulator). Vega is where Amazon is moving new hardware (Sept 2026), so a **Vega-native** app is better aligned with sponsor direction - but carries toolchain risk; Fire OS/Android TV emulator is the lower-risk path per hackathon FAQ.
- Strong pattern: TV = shared display, phones = private input (QR join), remote = always-works fallback, voice = optional. Show D-pad focus order, VoiceView labels, caption support and resume-after-Alexa in the demo.
- Avoid competing with Prime Video recaps / X-Ray / scene search; these are first-party and content-licensed.

### Why it could be hype
- Vega's app gap and enthusiast backlash (AFTVnews comments) suggest early-ecosystem risk; a small catalogue is opportunity for developers but also signals thin user base for non-streaming apps.
- Netflix's party-game push is a supply bet; reviewed sources contain no engagement data.
